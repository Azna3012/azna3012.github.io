Where the finance discipline came from — and where it's going

I didn't set out to become a specialist in any one function. My path — an MBA focused on Human Resources, a CMA (USA) qualification, early audit exposure, and close to two years teaching finance to CMA candidates — was built around a simpler question: how do organizations actually work, and what does it take to keep the people and the numbers moving in the same direction?

Auditing taught me to look for what's actually happening in a process, not just what's documented. Teaching cost management and corporate finance to adult learners — some preparing for the same exams I'd already cleared — taught me how to take something genuinely complicated and make it usable for someone under pressure to understand it fast. And coordinating a multi-team budget cycle for a global organization taught me that most of the real work in any business function is coordination: chasing the right input from the right person, at the right time, without letting anything fall through.

None of that is finance work in the narrow sense. It's the groundwork for project coordination, operations, and people leadership — which is the direction I'm building toward now.
